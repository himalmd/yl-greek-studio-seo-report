Why so many pages are excluded

We pulled the underlying GSC export for every exclusion reason. The two biggest buckets sound alarming but are non-issues; the third is where the real problem lives.

Resolved

340 pages "excluded by noindex tag"

▸

We exported the full URL list. 339 of 340 are /services/login_with_shop/authorize links — Shop Pay's login pre-qualification flow, which generates a fresh tracking ID on every load and is correctly auto-noindexed by Shopify. This is not a bug.

The one real product on the list, rho-pi-group-order-aka-knit-mesh-jersey, is worth a manual check — it looks like an unlisted "group order" link meant to be shared directly rather than surfaced in search, which is likely intentional.

Resolved

324 pages "blocked by robots.txt"

▸

The live robots.txt is essentially Shopify's stock file. It blocks collection sort/filter-combination URLs, cart, checkout, account, search, and the auto-generated /policies/ pages — all routine, correct exclusions. No misconfiguration found here.

Root cause

290 pages "crawled — currently not indexed"

▸

This is the bucket that reflects Google's actual quality judgment — and it traces to one structural issue, not weak content.

Composition of the 290 URLs

74% are the same products, duplicated across collection-scoped paths.

214 of 290 URLs (74%) are duplicates created by Shopify's internal linking pattern: the same product gets a distinct URL for every collection it's linked from — /collections/aka/products/x, /collections/new-arrivals/products/x, /collections/best-sellers/products/x — instead of always pointing to the clean /products/x. The canonical tag on the product template is correct, but Google still has to crawl every duplicate path before it can discard it, and it's spending crawl budget there instead of on unique pages.

57 unique products are affected across 30 different collections — several of which look like inactive seasonal campaigns still generating live, crawlable links.

Top collections generating duplicate paths

Collections with the most duplicate collection-scoped product URLs.
  • Remaining 76 URLs: 25 auto-generated collection .atom RSS feeds, 18 product URLs carrying Judge.me review or recommendation-widget tracking parameters, 17 paginated/filtered collection URLs, 5 direct product URLs that look like duplicate or orphaned listings (e.g. cropped-nylon-patchwork-bomber alongside the very similarly named delta-red-cropped-nylon-patch-bomber).

Confirmed by crawl. A Screaming Frog crawl from the homepage found 21 concrete examples of this exact pattern within a single click of the homepage — all under /collections/aka/products/... and /collections/delta-sigma-theta/products/..., each correctly canonicalizing back to /products/x. This pinpoints the fix: the AKA and DST collection page templates specifically are what's generating the duplicate links.

Major Issue

Enormous Network Payload & App Bloat (8.46 MB)

▸

A typical, well-optimized e-commerce homepage sits around 2.5MB to 3MB. YL Studio is downloading 8.46 MB of data via an astounding 419 HTTP requests. The majority of this weight is coming from 3.15 MB of JavaScript.

Looking at the waterfall chart, the site is saturated with heavy, unoptimized third-party Shopify apps. The browser is spending 6.8 seconds just trying to execute tasks on the main thread, stalling the page from becoming interactive. The heaviest offenders dragging down the site speed include:

  • PageFly (multiple sliders, helper, and slideshow scripts)
  • Klaviyo (tracking and embedded forms)
  • AddSauce (Shopify Instagram feed widgets)
  • Hextom / Vimotia (Video widgets)
  • RestockRocket (Back-in-stock notifications)

The Fix: Audit the Shopify app list immediately. Remove any apps no longer actively driving revenue. For essential apps, work with a developer to conditionally load their scripts (e.g., only load RestockRocket on product pages, not the homepage) or delay their execution until user interaction.

Fix

Hero image LCP is being lazy-loaded

▸

GTmetrix flagged that the Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) element—the main hero image—is being lazy-loaded. Lazy-loading is great for images further down the page, but telling the browser to delay loading the very first thing the user is supposed to see artificially slows down the perceived load time.

The Fix: Edit the hero banner section in the Shopify theme customization and ensure the first image has loading="eager" or fetchpriority="high" instead of loading="lazy".

Fix

Excessive DOM Size (4,448 elements)

▸

The browser has to render and map 4,448 HTML elements on the homepage (Google recommends staying below 1,500). An oversized DOM increases memory usage, causes longer style calculations, and produces costly layout reflows. This is almost certainly a byproduct of PageFly's drag-and-drop page builder architecture, which notoriously wraps elements in deep, nested <div> tags.

03

On-page SEO

Findings pulled directly from live homepage, collection, and product page content, plus a homepage-level Seobility scan.

Seobility on-page score — homepage

67% overall. Links and external factors are pulling the score down the most.
Confirms root cause

"Avoid internal links with dynamic URL parameters" — flagged as the top error

▸

This was Seobility's highest-priority, error-level flag on the homepage — an independent tool arriving at the same conclusion as the GSC analysis above. Internal links pointing to parameterized or collection-scoped URLs instead of clean canonical ones are the throughline connecting the indexation problem, the Links score (25%, the lowest of any category), and this finding.

Fix

H1 heading is too short and generic

▸

The homepage H1 reads "YL STUDIO LLC" — 13 characters, brand-only, below the ~20-character minimum Seobility recommends and carrying no descriptive or keyword value. There are also duplicate heading texts elsewhere on the page. Pairing the brand name with a short descriptor ("YL Studio — Custom Greek Apparel") would fix both the length and the missed keyword opportunity.

Fix

133 homepage images have no alt attribute

▸

Confirms the gap flagged in the first pass of this audit. Alt text is skipped across product imagery site-wide, which is common on Shopify since the platform doesn't auto-populate it — but it's a real loss for accessibility and image search, especially on a visually-driven apparel catalog.

Fix

Meta description exceeds recommended display width

▸

1,519px against a 1,000px maximum — about 50% over. This is the pixel-width version of the truncation issue flagged earlier: the description is long enough that Google cuts it in the SERP snippet regardless of character count. The meta title also has internal word repetition worth tightening.

Fix

DST product pages are titled "AKA" — a template copy-paste bug

▸

The Screaming Frog crawl caught a concrete mismatch: two Delta Sigma Theta products — black-dst-varsity-embroidered-patch-tee and dst-varsity-embroidered-patch-tee — both carry the title "YL STUDIO GREEK - AKA VARSITY PATCH TEE", identical to an actual AKA product's title tag. Separately, a DST short set (fortitude-signature-short-set) and an AKA short set (originalpinkset) share the exact same generic title, "YL STUDIO GREEK Original Signature Short Set."

This points to a product template being duplicated without the title field getting customized. It's a double problem: DST shoppers searching for DST-specific terms won't match on these titles, and it's a customer-facing accuracy issue on a store where organizational correctness matters to the audience.

Fix

Repeated boilerplate text across product pages

▸

Seobility found 6 duplicated text blocks on the homepage alone — the "final sale," "colors may differ from actual product," and sizing-disclaimer paragraphs, copied verbatim across listings. Fine as policy language, but worth trimming from the crawlable product description itself (move to a shared, non-duplicated snippet or the size-guide page) so it doesn't dilute unique on-page content site-wide.

Fix

Meta descriptions are truncating mid-sentence

▸

Product meta descriptions are auto-pulled from the first ~320 characters of body copy rather than hand-written. The AKA Knit Mesh Jersey listing cuts off at "Height: 5'1"" with trailing markup artifacts. This is very likely happening across most of the catalog and is wasting SERP click-through potential on every product page.

Fix

Product titles skip buyer-intent keywords

▸

Titles like "AKA KNIT MESH JERSEY" are clean but brand-only. Adding descriptive terms shoppers actually search — "sorority jersey," "Alpha Kappa Alpha apparel" (which commands ~2,400+ monthly searches), material or color — is a low-effort long-tail opportunity, especially paired with the authorized-vendor positioning.

Fix

Sold-out inventory dominates prime real estate

▸

Multiple homepage-featured and best-selling products are sold out. This isn't an indexation problem, but it hurts dwell time and conversion — both indirect ranking signals — and undercuts the impact of the "crawled, not indexed" fixes once traffic starts arriving.

Working well

Reviews, unique copy, and social proof

▸

488+ Judge.me reviews with star ratings surfaced directly in listings, genuinely unique (not manufacturer-boilerplate) product descriptions, and consistent brand voice — these are strong E-E-A-T signals and shouldn't be touched, just protected as the catalog structure gets cleaned up.

Investigate

Some top "backlinks" look like spam artifacts, not editorial links

▸

Moz's link data raises a flag beyond "thin backlinks." Several of the highest-authority referring pages read as spam-forum injections rather than genuine mentions — a Cornell University subdomain forum post (yambase-test.sgn.cornell.edu/forum/add_post.pl...) and multiple accounts.cancer.org login-redirect URLs that reference the domain in a query parameter rather than linking to it editorially.

More notable: Moz's "Top pages on this site" list includes a Russian-language URL (ylstudiogreek.com/список-записей-обновляемый/, roughly "list of updated records") and a page with an opaque encoded query string, both carrying real Page Authority and repeated anchor text pointing to them. A foreign-language slug appearing on an English apparel storefront is a common signature of either a compromised/injected page or a spam redirect exploiting the domain. Also worth a look: www.www.ylstudiogreek.com shows up as an indexed page variant, which points to a possible redirect misconfiguration.

Recommended next step: check the site directly for that Russian-language URL and the malformed www.www. variant, and if either resolves to real (unauthorized) content, treat it as a security issue first — clean it up, then follow with a Google disavow file for the spam-forum links once confirmed they aren't providing any value.
